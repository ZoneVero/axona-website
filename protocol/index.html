// Performance Protocol

Roles, not people

Version 1.0 · 14 November 2026

AXONA is a character-based performance project. SYNTHEX, NEURA, OVERCLOCK and GLITCH are roles within the AXONA world, embodied by authorized performers. The characters and their visual codes remain continuous; the cast may evolve by territory, production and era.

The voice you hear on the records is the voice. Songwriting, production and vocal identity are fixed in the recordings. A live performer interprets a role — they do not redefine it.

The public answer

SYNTHEX is the voice. Who carries it doesn't change what you hear.

01What stays the same, always

These are the character. No production, territory or cast changes them.

  • The stage name — SYNTHEX, NEURA, OVERCLOCK, GLITCH
  • The silhouette and mask
  • The accent colour in the visual code
  • The vocal timbre on all recorded material
  • The instrument each role carries
  • The archetype — how the character reads the world
  • The linguistic hook — how the character speaks

02What a performer brings

A performer brings interpretation, never canon.

Accumulates in the role

  • Stage presence and physical timing
  • Reading the room and reacting to it
  • Improvised ad-libs within the written line
  • Between-song patter, within agreed bounds
  • Mannerisms specific to an era

Never changes

  • Backstory facts
  • Relationships between characters
  • Base temperament
  • Vocal identity
  • Anything that enters the canon record

03The era log

Every performer hands over a written log when their engagement ends. It records what worked with audiences, which improvisations landed, what the character learned to be in that period, and notes for whoever carries the role next.

This is how a character grows without being rewritten. Each era makes the next one better instead of resetting it.

Era logs are strictly internal. They are not published. They exist so the role accumulates depth, not so the audience watches the machinery.

04Who is behind the mask

No performer is announced. No identity is confirmed or denied. This is not evasion — it is the same position held by every masked project that has lasted: the mask is the identity, and the person behind it is not part of the work.

What the audience watches is the role. What they remember is the character.

05Rotation

If and when roles rotate, the order is:

GLITCH  →  NEURA  →  OVERCLOCK  →  SYNTHEX

Synthex rotates last, because the voice is the hardest element to standardise and is the only role carrying lip-sync. Neura, Overclock and Glitch are standardisable through stems, controller templates and body mechanics.

Rotation is never complete. Each territory or production cycle keeps a stable primary cast, so audiences can build attachment and identify quality. Understudies rehearse privately and are not announced.

06Territory

Rehearsal: Romania. First shows: United Kingdom.

Romania is the lowest-cost place to rehearse a masked production — space, costume repair and crew. The United Kingdom is the primary reach market and where the first audience already exists. The two are separable and deliberately kept separate.

08Cosplay

Fan cosplay is welcome. Convention photos, personal content, costume builds for yourself — all encouraged, no permission needed.

Selling costumes, masks or replica props is not permitted without written agreement. This protects the visual code for everyone who builds one.

09Why the characters have no tragic backstory

They sing about digital fatigue. They do not need a reason. They are digitally tired.

A character who is sad because something happened to them is a victim — it asks for sympathy. A character who is sad because that is what they are is a character — it asks for interest. Sympathy runs out. Interest lasts.

The tension is not in the past. It is in the present: four people in the same room with the same machinery, who do not necessarily like each other, and cannot leave. Like a real band on tour. You do not need to know why they hate the road to see that they do.

10How fan feedback is used

Fans influence the character. They do not write it.

Scheduled review. Every six months, and before any new era, comments, emails, messages and replies are collected and analysed for themes, sentiment drift per character, and which language lands. The owner decides. Fans do not vote on personality.

Crossroad decisions. When there is a real fork with two or three defensible directions and the data cannot decide, the options are published with their real production cost, one question is asked in one place with a deadline, and both the result and the decision are published — including when the decision goes against the vote.

Hard limits. Fans do not decide canon facts, do not decide personality traits, do not see unreleased material in order to vote, and do not vote on commercial terms. No vote is binding.
